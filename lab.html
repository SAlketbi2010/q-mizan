How the models work

Simple physics, honest limits

Models A and B treat the stored carbon as one compact mass directly above or below the sensor, so the extra pull is Δg = G·M / r².

Model C spreads the carbon over 400 small masses in a growing cloud and adds up the vertical pull of each one at every point on the map, using Δg = G·m·z / (ρ² + z²)^1.5, where ρ is the sideways distance and z the vertical distance.

We count only the CO2 itself as added mass: we assume the injected seawater mostly pushes out water already in the rock.

What the models leave out

Real carbon spreads through cracks in shapes we cannot predict exactly. A real sensor must also be corrected for rain, Earth tides, temperature, air pressure and the mountains themselves. Because of this, our numbers are a first estimate to show the idea is plausible, not a design.
