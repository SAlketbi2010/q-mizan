What it would change

Fujairah’s rocks can lock CO2 away. Q-MIZAN could prove it, tonne by tonne.

For the UAE

Makes Fujairah’s carbon count

Independent, continuous proof for the UAE’s net-zero claims.

Trusted carbon credits for Fujairah’s cement plants and other heavy industry.

Emirati skills in quantum sensing, built with TII in Abu Dhabi.

For people

Keeps neighbours safe

Monitoring 24 hours a day instead of occasional water samples.

Early warning if injected fluid moves toward the wadi groundwater used by Fujairah’s farms.

Open data that builds public trust in carbon storage, and new green jobs on the East Coast.

For the world

Proof for carbon removal

Carbon removal only counts if it can be checked, and the world will need billions of tonnes of it.

The same weighing could work at peridotite and basalt sites worldwide, and at other CO2 stores.

A UAE-developed tool that makes carbon removal trustworthy enough to scale.

Honest limits

Today’s pilots are too small to see: 300 t of carbon drops below the sensor limit beyond about 20 m. A quantum sensor slim enough for a borehole does not exist yet. Mountain terrain, rain and tides must be corrected for. Gravity shows the mass has stopped moving; water chemistry from wells is still needed to confirm it has become rock.

Quantum gravimeters already work in the field, and gravity has tracked stored CO2 elsewhere. Weighing mineralised carbon in peridotite has never been tried. We have a design, physics estimates and simulations.

Next steps
  1. Use 44.01’s injection records and the site’s geology to model the expected gravity change in Fujairah.
  2. Place a field quantum gravimeter at the site for 12 months: does gravity rise in step with tonnes injected?
  3. Design and lab-test a slim borehole version of the sensor.
  4. Correct for mountain terrain, rain and tides, and test in summer heat.
What we would need

Partners: TII Quantum Research Center (sensors), 44.01, FNRC, the Fujairah Environment Authority and ADNOC (site, data, boreholes), Khalifa University geoscientists.

Equipment and funding: a field quantum gravimeter, access to a monitoring borehole, precise GPS for height (1 cm of height changes gravity by about 3 µGal), a solar enclosure, and a one-year pilot budget.
